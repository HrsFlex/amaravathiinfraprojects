Understand the evidence.
Know the assumptions.

Amaravati has a substantial planned infrastructure and institutional-finance programme. That is a basis for further property research. It does not establish that every plot is investable, that prices will rise, or that Amaravati is the best investment for every buyer.

No reliable, comparable locality-level transaction-price series was established in this research. This website therefore does not present a historical property-growth chart or map of expected local ROI. The calculator is a scenario tool using assumptions selected by the visitor.

5. Investment-planner methodology

The visitor sets purchase price P, holding period n, annual price change r, entry-cost percentage e and exit-cost percentage x. All percentages are inputs, not observed Amaravati market rates or quoted statutory charges.

  • Total initial outlay = P × (1 + e/100)
  • Gross modelled resale value = P × (1 + r/100)ⁿ
  • Net resale proceeds = gross resale value × (1 − x/100)
  • Net gain/loss = net resale proceeds − initial outlay
  • Net total return = (net gain/loss ÷ initial outlay) × 100

Example: at ₹25 lakh, 5 years, 6% annual price change, 8% entry costs and 2% exit costs, the initial outlay is ₹27.00 lakh. Modelled net proceeds are ₹32.79 lakh; modelled gain is ₹5.79 lakh and total return is approximately 21.4%. These are nominal, unlevered scenario values.

The chart compares −5%, 0%, the selected assumption and 12% annual change. These are illustrative input cases. They are not risk categories, probability estimates, analyst forecasts or measured historical growth rates. The chart displays gross property value, while the summary includes the selected transaction-cost assumptions.

Excluded: loan interest, financing fees, taxes, rental income, vacancy, development and maintenance costs, inflation, illiquidity and the possibility of not finding a buyer. Real results may be materially different and losses are possible.
